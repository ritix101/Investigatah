One director, 120+ companies, 150+ gambling sites, and a deepfake app

Who sits behind a cluster of skin-betting and casino sites with no visible owner, and what else do they run?

I mapped one Cyprus-based director to 120+ registered companies. At least five are linked to 150+ casino and skin-betting sites. One of them, Crystal Future OÜ, is also named as operator of BikiniOff, an AI app that turns ordinary photos of real people into fake nude images.

The topskin.cc domain record names the director, through Crystal Future OÜ, as owner. That confirmed the link independently.

Findings, method and deliverable

Findings

  • Deterministic

    One person holds founder, director or shareholder roles in 120+ Cyprus companies, active since January 2017.Company registries

  • Probabilistic

    Four companies form the operating backbone, linked to 150+ casino and skin-betting sites and a fantasy-football app with 1M+ downloads.Site T&Cs, footers, app store

  • Deterministic

    The topskin.cc domain record names the same person, through Crystal Future OÜ, as owner.Domain record

  • Deterministic

How I worked

  1. Collected operating-company names from site footers, terms and payment pages.
  2. Searched company registries for directors and shareholders.
  3. Confirmed the link through a domain record naming the same person.
  4. Used archived pages for sites that had changed or gone offline, including the deleted bikinioff.net.
  5. Read BikiniOff's archived user agreement for consent, moderation and age checks.
  6. Checked legal history, including a WIPO domain dispute.

Delivered

Network map linking person → companies → sites → AI app, with a source for every link.

Rollbit

Casino-funded influencer wallets

Were the streamers and influencers promoting Rollbit betting their own money, or the casino's?

I mapped 15+ wallets around a high-profile streamer. Deposits he presented as his own purchases came from Rollbit's hot wallet minutes earlier, and went straight back to the casino.

350.9 ETHSent by the casino 5 minutes before a stream
15+Wallets mapped around one streamer
~$90METH moved from casino hot wallets to exchange deposits
Evidence chain
  1. Rollbit hot wallet0xCBD6…75ac
  2. 350.915 ETH · 5 min before stream
  3. Streamer exchange deposit0x24AF…7dC7
  4. Deposited to casino
  5. Streamer casino deposit0xd267…ebB7

21 Dec 2021. The streamer went live showing a ~$1M Rollbit balance minutes after this transfer.

Findings, method and deliverable

Findings

  • Deterministic

    Rollbit's hot wallet sent 350.915 ETH to the streamer's exchange deposit address five minutes before he went live showing a ~$1M balance.Transfer history, stream time

  • Deterministic

    86.9 ETH arrived from the casino 27 minutes before he posted that he had personally bought ~$170K of ETH. The ETH then went back to Rollbit.Transfer history, archived post

  • Heuristic

    The repeated timing fits sponsored play money presented as personal funds.Pattern across events

  • Deterministic

    Casino hot wallets held ~$10M of ETH while ~$90M had been sent to two exchange deposit addresses.Balances and outflows

  • Probabilistic

    A second influencer (280K followers) bought RLB, deposited it to his own casino deposit address, withdrew to a fresh wallet and sold. At least 9 transfers (30,000 SOL) from the selling wallet back to that unique deposit address tie both wallets to him.Deposit-address reuse

  • Reported

    He was publicly accused of selling ~45M RLB (~$3M at peak) while promoting it.Public allegation

How I worked

  1. Labelled Rollbit's main hot wallet and the streamer's exchange and casino deposit addresses.
  2. Matched his public posts and stream start times against transfer timestamps.
  3. Followed every deposit forward to see where the funds ended up.
  4. Compared what the casino hot wallets hold with what they sent to exchange deposit addresses.
  5. Reviewed a public pump-and-dump allegation against a second influencer and mapped its four-wallet loop.

Delivered

Evidence pack with transaction hashes, timestamped screenshots and a fund-flow graph, written so a non-technical reader can follow each step. The findings were published as part of a public report.

Sources and toolsArkham · Etherscan · Solscan · Archived posts

Luck.io

Is "self-custody" really self-custody?

Are Luck.io's "self-custodial" Smart Vaults really controlled only by the player?

No. Every vault is a program-owned account. The program is upgradeable, and its upgrade key sits with a 3-of-9 anonymous multisig. The audited code also could not be matched to what is deployed.

3 of 9Anonymous signatures can change vault logic
1Program owns every user's vault
0Public source code
Evidence chain
  1. User Smart VaultPDA, no private key
  2. Owned by
  3. Vault programAQZd…pYom
  4. Upgrade authority
  5. Squads V4 multisig3-of-9, anonymous

Whoever controls the upgrade authority can redeploy the program and change how vaults behave, including withdrawals.

Findings, method and deliverable

Findings

  • Deterministic

    Vaults are program-derived addresses (isOnCurve = false), so they have no private key and the user cannot sign for them.Account data

  • Deterministic

    Every vault checked, including my own test vault, is owned by one program.Owner field

  • Deterministic

    The program is upgradeable, and its authority is a 3-of-9 Squads V4 multisig with anonymous signers.Upgrade authority

  • Deterministic

    Source code is not public, and the audited build's hash does not match the deployed program.Verification API

  • Reported

    Wins and losses are settled by backend wallets, and randomness is computed off-chain. I found no oracle list and no on-chain randomness proofs.Halborn audit, tx review

How I worked

  1. Deposited to a test vault and inspected the account on-chain (on-curve check, owner field).
  2. Traced ownership up to the program and its upgrade authority.
  3. Compared the audited code hash with the deployed program using OtterSec's verification API.
  4. Read the Halborn audit to see which actions happen off-chain.
  5. Tested each marketing claim against what the chain shows.

Delivered

A claim-by-claim verdict table (true, partly true, unverifiable, false) a non-technical reader can use. Bottom line: the custody and fairness claims cannot be verified on-chain.

Sources and toolsSolscan · Solana account data · OtterSec verify API · Halborn audit

BetBolt

Why the casino's domain went dark

Why did a crypto casino's main domain go offline, and does its license claim hold up?

WHOIS showed the registrar had put the domain on clientHold, the status a registrar uses to disable a domain. The registrar's legal team cited legal and operational issues, and the casino moved to a new domain at another registrar. Its license claims didn't hold up either.

4d 19hOffline when I started
clientHoldStatus set by the registrar
0Verifiable gambling licenses
Evidence chain
  1. betbolt.comOffline
  2. WHOIS status
  3. clientHoldSet by registrar
  4. Registrar legal team
  5. betbolt.ioNew registrar

clientHold is set by the registrar, not the site owner. Most likely cause: a legal order served on the registrar.

Findings, method and deliverable

Findings

  • Deterministic

    Domain status was clientHold, set by the registrar (Namecheap).WHOIS

  • Reported

    The registrar's legal team cited legal and operational issues.Email reply

  • Probabilistic

    Most likely cause: a legal order served on the registrar.Combined evidence

  • Deterministic

    The site moved to betbolt.io under a different registrar.WHOIS

  • Probabilistic

    The Costa Rica "license" is a data-processing registration, not a gambling license. The claimed Curaçao move is unverified.Public records

  • Reported

    Users reported withdrawals delayed 15+ days.User complaints

How I worked

  1. Listed possible causes: seizure, registrar dispute, trademark claim, blacklisting, DNS fault.
  2. Checked WHOIS status codes and DNS records.
  3. Contacted the registrar's legal team.
  4. Tracked the move to the new domain and registrar.
  5. Reviewed license claims and the platform's history, including the move from India-only UPI and Paytm payments (2023) to crypto-only (late 2024).
  6. Linked the anonymous CEO account to a real person through its first username, an early Discord admin role and UK company filings.

Delivered

Cause-of-outage verdict with a timeline, a license assessment and a founder profile.

Rollbit

RLB token supply and treasury audit

Was RLB's "fully circulating supply" claim true, and where did the team's tokens go?

I traced RLB from mint to burn on two chains. A dormant wallet holding 50M RLB was activated with gas from Rollbit's own wallets, then sold everything for $1.43M. After the move to Ethereum, Rollbit wallets still held about 60% of circulating supply.

5BRLB minted on Solana, 23 Nov 2021
$1.43MSold from a team-linked wallet
59.6%Of circulating supply held by Rollbit wallets
Evidence chain
  1. Rollbit treasury
  2. 50M RLB · 26 Nov 2021
  3. Dormant wallet5E2e…Kxnr
  4. Sold on OpenBook
  5. $1.43M USDT

Gas to wake the wallet in Jan 2023 came from the deployer (0.1 SOL) and Rollbit's RLB deposit wallet (1.51 SOL). That link ties it to the team.

Findings, method and deliverable

Findings

  • Deterministic

    5B RLB minted in 5 transactions. 4.8B went to the treasury, and 96.4% of that sat in one centralized hot wallet.Mint and transfer records

  • Deterministic

    50M RLB moved to a separate wallet that stayed dormant until Jan 2023, when the deployer and the RLB deposit wallet paid its gas.Gas-funding transfers

  • Deterministic

    That wallet sold all 50M RLB on OpenBook by 16 Feb 2023 for $1.43M USDT and turned $300K into 13,000 SOL.DEX trade history

  • Deterministic

    The liquidity wallet pulled 3M RLB + 7,661 USDT from the pool (Nov 2022), then sold on Orca for 1.74M USDC (Aug 2023).Pool and swap records

  • Deterministic

    On Ethereum: deployer holds 1.1B (22%), 2.9B (57%) is burned, and 59.6% of the ~2.1B circulating sits in Rollbit wallets. Only ~1.2% is in the main pool, so a $500K sale moves price ~13%.Holder balances, pool depth

  • Deterministic

    Jan–Nov 2024: 895.79M RLB burned, while 895.68M was sent to the hot wallet over the same period.Token transfer totals

  • Reported

    No updated tokenomics were published after migration, despite a promised breakdown on 1 May 2024.Public posts

How I worked

  1. Rebuilt the Solana mint: deployer, treasury, airdrop, liquidity wallet and a fifth wallet funded three days after launch.
  2. Followed gas funding to tie dormant wallets back to the team.
  3. Tracked liquidity removal and sales on Raydium, Orca and OpenBook.
  4. Reconciled burns and the Ethereum migration against the claimed supply.
  5. Measured pool depth and the price impact of a large sale.

Delivered

Wallet map from mint to burn, a supply reconciliation table and a timeline. Together they show the "fully circulating" claim does not match on-chain holdings.

Sources and toolsSolscan · Etherscan · Arkham · DEX trade data

Method

How I grade evidence

Every finding in a report says how sure I am and why. A reader can see at a glance what is proven and what is still a lead — and nothing gets stated more confidently than its source allows.

Deterministic

Directly visible on-chain or in a primary record — a transfer, a registry entry, a domain record.

Probabilistic

A strong attribution built from several linked pieces of evidence that independently point the same way.

Heuristic

A pattern-based inference, such as repeated timing or wallet behaviour. Useful, but always flagged as such.

Reported

A third-party statement: an audit, a court filing, news coverage or user complaints.

  1. Pin down the questionWho runs it, where did the money go, is the claim true. One question per report.
  2. Collect primary recordsTransactions, registries, domain records, terms, archived pages. Saved with dates.
  3. Pivot on indicatorsA username, a wallet, a company number, a footer. Each one leads to the next.
  4. Verify each linkA second, independent source before a link is treated as solid.
  5. Grade and limitTag each finding by confidence and write down what the evidence cannot show.
  6. Report in plain wordsFund-flow maps, timelines and verdict tables a non-technical reader can follow.
